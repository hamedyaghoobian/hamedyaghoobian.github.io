What Rumi Recited

The Quran in the Divan-e Shams / 2026

Ayahs as threads. 809 couplets in 609 poems, 286 ayahs from 94 surahs

Also show

Rumi, مولانا جلال‌الدین محمد بلخی, was a preacher and a teacher of law in Konya before he was a poet. In 1244 he met a wandering dervish, Shams of Tabriz, and the meeting turned him to poetry. Many of the lyrics he wrote over the next thirty years he signed with his friend's name, and they were gathered as the Divan-e Shams, دیوان شمس: on Ganjoor, 3,229 ghazals, 44 strophic poems and 1,995 quatrains. The Quran, the preacher's book, runs all through them.

This is every couplet in the Divan found to quote, paraphrase or allude to a particular ayah: 809 couplets in 609 poems. Along the top are the Quran's 6,236 ayahs, in the order of the book; along the foot, the Divan's 5,268 poems, in the order Ganjoor gives them, which is not the order Rumi wrote them in but the order of their rhymes. Each couplet is a thread from the ayah to the poem. Press play and the Divan is read through from its first ghazal to its last quatrain, and each thread is drawn as its couplet is reached. An ayah grows as it is heard again.

Most of the Quran is never heard. 286 ayahs are, from 94 of its 114 surahs, and 171 of them only once. A few are heard over and over, and they are the stories. The ayah heard most is the covenant before creation, 7:172, when God asks the unborn souls أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ, am I not your Lord?, and they answer بَلَىٰ, yes: 65 couplets. Then Moses on the mountain, 7:143, asking to see God and told لَن تَرَىٰنِى, you will not see me, before the mountain falls to dust: 50. Then the women who cut their hands at the sight of Joseph, 12:31; the two in the cave, 9:40; the verse of light, 24:35; and the fire made cool for Abraham, 21:69. One ghazal, 1872, ends every one of its eleven couplets on the same command, رو کم ترکوا برخوان, go and recite how many they left behind: the gardens and springs of 44:25, left to others by the proud who died.

The couplets were found by Saber Soleymani's pipeline, which screened the Divan through Ganjoor's commentaries, matched each candidate against the whole Quran and kept a link only when the couplet's own words bore it out. 413 of the links quote the ayah's words, 56 paraphrase them and 340 allude to its story or its image. A second, blind review, by a language model shown each couplet with the cited ayah shuffled among its neighbours and look-alikes, picked that ayah, and only that ayah, 792 times. The other 17, the doubtful, are drawn with the rest; ask for them and they are marked. What each ayah is about, and the short gloss on how each couplet takes it up, were written by a language model too; they are marked as its.

Press play, or space; ← and → go to the poem before or after, 1, 2 and 3 to the ghazals, the strophic poems and the quatrains, and 0 to the end. Point at a thread, or press j and k to go through the couplets in the Divan's order. Point at an ayah to read it and everything that answers it, then j and k go through those alone.

The citations: Saber Soleymani, shams-quran-link, from the Divan's text and commentaries on Ganjoor, with Hamed Yaghoobian's review and annotations. The Quran's text: the Uthmani text of Tanzil, through alquran.cloud; the Persian translation, Mohammad-Mahdi Fooladvand's. The themes, figures, relations and glosses were written by Claude Opus 5.5 and checked by a second, blind pass of the same model. Made in 2026 with Claude, a language model.

Code and data: github.com/noghte/shams-quran-link
