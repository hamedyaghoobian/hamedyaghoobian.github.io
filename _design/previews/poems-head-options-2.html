the coda: the last entry, with no dash

Each option takes the dash out and lets the layout do its work. All four keep the frame of an open poem: the ink rule (or a ground line) and Darwish signed in Persian.

i · lineated, measuredBroken into three lines like the poems above it, each stepping in and carrying its letter count in mono, as the figure counts every line. Darwish gets read the way the page reads everything else.

and last

I’ve built my homeland,18

I’ve even founded my state21

in my language.12

محمود درویش mahmoud darwish

ii · on its groundBuilt from the bottom up. "In my language" is the ground line, larger and ruled underneath; the homeland and the state stand on it in the softer ink.

and last

I’ve built my homeland,

I’ve even founded my state

in my language.

محمود درویش mahmoud darwish

iii · the language changesThe sentence runs in English until its last words, which arrive in Persian (در زبانم, "in my language"). The line does what it says.

and last

I’ve built my homeland, I’ve even founded my state در زبانمدر زبانم · in my language

محمود درویش mahmoud darwish

iv · drawn, not filledHomeland and state are set as outlines, like plans not yet built; only "language" is solid ink. The one place that exists is the language.

and last

I’ve built my homeland, I’ve even founded my state in my language.

محمود درویش mahmoud darwish
