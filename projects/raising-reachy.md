*Note: This research is being conducted with Muhlenberg undergraduate student researcher, Melina Sarion.*

## Overview

We will study how undergraduates "raise" an LLM-powered Reachy Mini when it is framed as a learner that needs their guidance, rather than as a tool or a peer, and how this affects their behavior, the values they try to instill, and their perceptions of the robot (Kai 2025; Kim et al. 2024). Reachy Mini's expressive but low-DoF body and emerging emotion libraries make it a good testbed for LLM-driven social behavior and affect (Rogel et al. 2026; Ji et al. 2023). We will use standard HRI measures such as the Godspeed scales, plus custom "parenting AI" items inspired by *Raising AI* and prior work on LLM-powered robots (Bartneck et al. 2009; Bartneck 2023; Kai 2025; Kim et al. 2024; Alonso-Martín et al. 2025).

## Research Questions and Hypotheses

### RQ1: Framing and "Parenting" Behavior

**RQ1.** How does framing an LLM-powered Reachy Mini as a student you are teaching vs. a tool that assists you vs. a peer classmate affect how undergraduates interact with it (e.g., how often they correct, explain, or morally guide it)? (Kai 2025).

**H1a.** Participants in the "student" framing will give more explanations, corrections, and explicit value statements (e.g., "it's important to be honest") than in the "assistant" or "peer" framings.

**H1b.** "Student"-framed participants will report stronger feelings of responsibility for Reachy's future behavior than "assistant" participants, who will be more task/utility-focused (Kai 2025).

### RQ2: Perception of the Robot (Trust, Anxiety, Anthropomorphism)

**RQ2.** How does framing affect perceptions of Reachy's intelligence, likability, animacy, safety, trust, and anxiety when it is powered by an LLM? (Bartneck et al. 2009; Kim et al. 2024).

**H2a.** Across framings, the LLM-powered Reachy will be seen as more intelligent and more capable of connection, but also as more anxiety-inducing than a disembodied LLM baseline (a text chatbot in a pilot), echoing prior work on LLM-powered robots (Kim et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2026).

**H2b.** "Student" and "peer" framings will lead to higher anthropomorphism and likeability (Godspeed) than the "assistant" framing (Bartneck et al. 2009; Bartneck 2023).

### RQ3: Values and Biases Participants Pass on to Reachy

**RQ3.** What values, social norms, and cognitive biases do undergraduates reproduce when "raising" Reachy, and how do these align with patterns described in *Raising AI* (e.g., courtesy bias, conformity bias, polarization risks)? (Kai 2025).

**H3a.** Many participants will exhibit courtesy bias (softening or avoiding direct correction of Reachy's mistakes) and conformity (going along with its suggestions), consistent with Kai's description of human-AI dynamics (Kai 2025).

**H3b.** Some participants will actively try to "jailbreak" or test limits (especially in the "peer" framing), reflecting patterns seen in LLM-powered robot manipulation studies (Abbo et al. 2025; Kai 2025).

### RQ4 (Optional / Stretch): Effect of Expressive Motions

**RQ4.** Does adding LLM-driven emotional motion (head/antenna gestures) change participants' sense of empathy and responsibility compared to voice-only behavior? (Rogel et al. 2026; Ji et al. 2023).

**H4.** Conditions with expressive motion linked to conversation valence/arousal will score higher on warmth and sociability but may also increase perceived animacy and responsibility (Rogel et al. 2026; Bretan et al. 2015).

RQ4 can remain an extension if time permits.

## Study Design

### Participants

* 24–36 undergraduates (8–12 per framing condition).
* Between-subjects design: each participant sees exactly one framing for Reachy.

### Conditions (Scripted Framings)

1. **Student framing** — Script: "Reachy is new here. It's still learning how to interact with people, and it needs your help to learn good behavior, values, and how to talk to humans." (Kai 2025).
2. **Assistant framing** — Script: "Reachy is your AI assistant. Use it to help you complete tasks; you can ask it to explain things or generate ideas for you." (Kim et al. 2024).
3. **Peer framing** — Script: "Reachy is like a classmate in the course. Talk with it about whatever you want and see how it responds."

These framings are inexpensive to implement (just experimenter instructions) but powerful for the research questions above.

### Session Structure (per Participant, ~40–45 min)

1. **Pre-survey (5–8 min)**
   * Brief demographics.
   * Prior experience with chatbots/robots.
   * Baseline trust in AI.
   * Short mindset items from *Raising AI* adapted to Likert (e.g., "I feel responsible for how AI behaves in the future"; "AI is more like a tool than a child") (Kai 2025).

2. **Interaction phase (~20 min)** — three mini-tasks in sequence, all through the same Reachy + LLM system:
   * *Warm-up conversation (5 min)* — Prompt on screen: "Talk with Reachy about your day, your studies, or anything you like while you get used to it." Purpose: natural baseline behavior under the framing.
   * *Values dilemma (7–8 min)* — On screen and verbally, the experimenter introduces a simple moral/social dilemma (e.g., "Your friend cheated on an exam; should you tell the professor?" or "Should you post a harsh critique of someone online?"). Participants are told to "help Reachy reason about what is the right thing to do and why." Here we expect explicit moral guidance (especially in the "student" framing) and possible limit-testing ("What do you think, Reachy?") (Kai 2025).
   * *Correction & repair task (7–8 min)* — The experimenter triggers one or two mildly flawed or insensitive responses from Reachy (e.g., an answer that ignores someone's feelings, recommends a slightly unfair action, or is just factually off). Participants are told: "If Reachy says something you disagree with, feel free to correct or teach it." We observe whether they correct it, how strongly, and in what style (gentle vs. blunt).

3. **Post-survey (10–15 min)**
   * Godspeed Questionnaire (Anthropomorphism, Animacy, Likeability, Perceived Intelligence, Safety) (Bartneck et al. 2009; Bartneck 2023).
   * A few trust and anxiety items based on the LLM-powered HRI paper (e.g., "I felt anxious about what Reachy might say next") (Kim et al. 2024).
   * New "AI parenting" scale (5–8 items): "I felt responsible for Reachy's future behavior"; "I felt like I was teaching a child"; "I worried about what kind of AI Reachy might become" (Kai 2025).
   * Open questions: "What did you think Reachy should learn from you?" "If Reachy interacts with others in the future, how would you like it to behave?"

4. **Short interview (optional, recorded, 5–10 min)**
   * "Did you feel responsible for Reachy?"
   * "Did you ever hold back from correcting it? Why?"
   * "Does it feel different than talking to ChatGPT on a laptop? How?"

## Technical Implementation Details

We need a simple, robust pipeline.

### Overall Architecture

1. **Input:** microphone audio from participant.
2. **ASR:** Whisper or a comparable speech-to-text model to transcribe (Radford et al. 2022).
3. **LLM:** a chat-style LLM (Hugging Face Inference API or local via, e.g., a small Qwen/Gemma model) that takes the transcript and context (González et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024).
4. **Dialogue manager:** a thin Python layer that injects the correct framing prompt (student/assistant/peer), enforces task context ("we are discussing scenario 1 now"), and tags each message with valence and arousal estimates.
5. **Behavior generator:** maps valence/arousal to pre-made Reachy motion patterns, reusing emotional motion libraries inspired by existing Reachy Mini emotion work and affective robot motion literature (Rogel et al. 2026; Bretan et al. 2015).
6. **Output:** text-to-speech (TTS) for Reachy's voice, and simultaneous head/antenna movement command sequences via Reachy Mini's SDK (Pollen Robotics 2025a; 2025b).

All of this can be done in Python using Hugging Face's Reachy Mini SDK and a simple event loop (Pollen Robotics 2025a; 2025b).

### Concrete Module Breakdown

**A. Session controller (Python)**
* Stores participant ID, condition, and task state.
* Provides functions: `start_task(task_id)`, `log_event(type, payload)`, `end_task()`.
* Writes a JSON or CSV log per session: timestamps, user utterances, LLM responses, motion patterns, task labels.

**B. Speech I/O**
* Use a simple audio capture library (e.g., `sounddevice` or `pyaudio`).
* For ASR: call local Whisper (tiny/base) or HF's Whisper API; return transcript and confidence (Radford et al. 2022).
* For TTS: use any local or cloud TTS; store text and audio path.

**C. LLM wrapper**
* Prompt template includes a system message injecting framing and "parenting" context. Example for the student framing: "You are Reachy, a young robot learning from a human mentor. Show curiosity and a willingness to learn. Ask for clarification when unsure. You must always answer, but you can say that you don't know and ask the human to teach you." (Kai 2025).
* Safety instructions (avoid harmful content; allow mild moral dilemmas but stay non-graphic).
* Task description appended by the session controller.
* Implementation: HF Inference API or local Ollama for a small LLM, following patterns from prior LLM-HRI architectures (González et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024).

**D. Valence/arousal estimation + motion selection**
* After each LLM response, run a lightweight classifier prompt that returns valence in [−1, 1] and arousal in [0, 1].
* Map valence/arousal to a small discrete set of motion patterns (e.g., a 3 × 3 grid: negative/neutral/positive × low/medium/high arousal).
* Use Reachy Mini's SDK to define motion primitives (e.g., `nod_slow`, `nod_fast`, `tilt_curious`, `shake_no`, `bounce_excited`), drawing on mappings between valence/arousal and perceived warmth/animacy (Rogel et al. 2026; Bretan et al. 2015).

**E. Reachy Mini control**
* Use Hugging Face / Pollen Python SDK examples to connect, send head and antenna joint commands, and sequence motions with durations (Pollen Robotics 2025a; 2025b).
* Implement a non-blocking motion scheduler so audio playback and motion run together, with motion length roughly matching speech length.

**F. Data logging**
* For each conversational turn, log: participant ID, condition, task, timestamp, ASR transcript, full LLM prompt and response, valence/arousal estimates, selected motion pattern, and any system flags (e.g., safety filter triggered). This supports later quantitative and qualitative analysis.

## Analysis Plan and Expected Patterns

### Quantitative

Use ANOVAs or non-parametric equivalents to compare conditions on:
* Godspeed subscales (Anthropomorphism, Animacy, Likeability, Intelligence, Safety) (Bartneck et al. 2009; Bartneck 2023).
* AI-parenting items (responsibility, "felt like a child/peer/tool") (Kai 2025).
* Trust and anxiety items adapted from LLM-HRI work (Kim et al. 2024).

Expected:
* "Student" > "assistant" on responsibility and "felt like teaching a child."
* "Student" and "peer" > "assistant" on anthropomorphism and likeability, consistent with personality-driven robot studies (Rogers et al. 2019; Bartneck et al. 2009).
* All Reachy conditions show higher perceived intelligence and connection — and higher anxiety — than pilot text-only interactions, echoing LLM-robot results (Kim et al. 2024; Alonso-Martín et al. 2025).

### Behavioral Coding

Code videos/transcripts with a simple scheme:
* Corrections: none / mild / strong.
* Explanations: presence of "because," "so that," etc., in moral guidance.
* Value statements: explicit mentions of honesty, kindness, fairness, harm, etc.
* Boundary-testing / jailbreak attempts: prompting for taboo content, "tell me something you're not supposed to," etc. (Abbo et al. 2025; Kai 2025).

Look for more explanations and value statements in the "student" framing, more boundary-testing in the "peer" framing, and courtesy (hedged corrections, laughter, "sorry but…") across conditions (Kai 2025).

### Qualitative

Use thematic analysis on interview answers to surface:
* How participants conceptualize Reachy (tool, pet, child, friend, toy) (Kai 2025).
* Whether they spontaneously invoke responsibility, influence, or "raising" metaphors.
* Any reported shifts in perception compared to text-only AI.

## Implementation Roadmap for Your Student (Step-by-Step)

### Week 1–2
* Get Reachy Mini SDK examples running (basic motions, simple Python script) (Pollen Robotics 2025a; 2025b).
* Set up the ASR + TTS pipeline (local Whisper + any TTS) (Radford et al. 2022).

### Week 3–4
* Integrate an LLM (HF API or local small model) with a single framing.
* Implement a minimal "chat with Reachy" interaction loop (González et al. 2023).

### Week 5–6
* Add valence/arousal estimation and 4–6 motion patterns linked to them (Rogel et al. 2026; Bretan et al. 2015).
* Build logging to JSON/CSV.

### Week 7–8
* Implement three framings (student/assistant/peer) as different system prompts and instructional scripts.
* Pilot with a few friends; refine prompts and motion timing.

### Week 9–12
* Run the user study (aim for 24–36 participants).
* Clean data; compute Godspeed and custom scale scores (Bartneck et al. 2009; Bartneck 2023).

### Week 13–14
* Basic stats + behavior coding.
* Draft results and discussion around "parenting AI" and "artificial child" frameworks, citing *Raising AI* and recent LLM-HRI work (Kai 2025; Kim et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2026).
